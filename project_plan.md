# Project plan: "bank" flight mode

A new flight mode, **"bank"**. Development conventions (target independence, compile-time gating, tests, PR rules) are in [AGENTS.md](AGENTS.md).

Goal: fly a drone like a car on a banked track. The three control axes are coupled down to two, with a spare stick axis for look-around.

## Terms

Agreed with the owner on 2026-10-16. Use these words, in these meanings, when talking about the mode, in code comments and in reviews.

**Two frames of reference.**
- **World frame**: fixed to the ground. Up is against gravity, and the horizontal plane is the plane the craft drives in, like the surface of a banked track. Bank mode is mostly defined here: the pilot asks for what the craft should do in the world, not for an attitude.
- **Drone frame** (body frame): fixed to the craft. Its axes are the nose (forward), the left side and the top, so it tilts and turns with the craft. The props push along the drone frame's top axis, and the accelerometer and gyro measure in this frame.

**The three controls (world frame).** In bank mode the sticks are called by what they control, not by their channel names:
- **Speed** (left stick up and down, the throttle channel): forward motion along the direction of travel, horizontal in the world frame. The lower half of the stick asks for a forward *velocity*: the mode estimates the speed from the rotor drag the accelerometer measures and drives it to the stick with a speed loop, so Speed at the bottom brakes to a stop, relative to the air (the same as relative to the ground when there is no wind). The upper half asks for a forward *force*, from what the centre gives up to all the thrust allows at the top. No reverse.
- **Forward force**: the forward, horizontal, world-frame component of the thrust, in g. The speed loop sets it in the lower half of the Speed stick; the stick sets it directly in the upper half.
- **Lift** (right stick up and down, the pitch channel): the vertical force in the world frame, in multiples of the craft's weight (g). It is a force, not a speed or a height: 1 g at the centre balances the weight, so the craft has no vertical acceleration, and whatever vertical velocity it already has carries on. See "Lift stays a force" below.
- **Bank** (left stick left and right, the yaw channel): the bank angle of the turn, and with it the sideways force in the world frame that curves the track. The nose is turned to follow the turn by itself (the ball, below), and looks into the turn, further the more Bank against Speed (see "Looking into the turn").
- **Head check** (right stick left and right, the roll channel): turns the nose away from the direction of travel without changing the direction of travel. It is the one control that is about the drone frame: it moves the nose, not the track.

**Thrust (drone frame).** The absolute force out of the props, along the drone frame's top axis. It is the one force the craft can make, so Lift, the forward force that holds the Speed and the sideways force of the Bank are all components of the thrust, seen in the world frame. Tilting the craft points the thrust: tilting forward by the pitch θ turns part of it into forward force, banking by φ turns part of it sideways, and what is left points up as Lift:

    Thrust = Lift / (cos θ · cos φ)

So with Lift at 1 g and Speed pushed up, the craft pitches forward and the thrust rises by 1 / cos θ so that Lift stays at 1 g. The same formula holds at every Speed, Lift and Bank.

**Other words used in this plan and in `bank_mode.c`.**
- **Collective**: the command to the motors, 0 to 1, that sets the thrust (the same for all motors before the attitude loop adds its differences). The mode turns a thrust into a collective with the thrust curve `thrust = bank_thrust_to_weight * collective^1.5`.
- **Thrust to weight** (T/W, `bank_thrust_to_weight`): the thrust at full collective, in multiples of the weight. Fixed by a setting, measured in a hover.
- **Thrust trim**: a factor on the modelled thrust, measured in flight from the accelerometer's vertical force against the Lift asked for. Real props make more or less thrust per collective than the fixed model (more when braking into the air, much less in fast forward flight), and the trim corrects for that, so the Lift asked for is the Lift the craft gets.
- **g of drag**: the unit of the speed estimate. The rotor drag, in g, the craft would have at that speed at 1 g of thrust. In steady flight it is the forward force that holds the speed, which is why no m/s calibration is needed. On the simulator's five inch 1.0 g of drag is about 40 m/s.
- **Direction of travel** (track, travel heading): the direction the craft moves in the horizontal plane. Speed is along it, Bank curves it, and the head check offsets the nose from it.
- **The ball**: the sideways force the accelerometer measures in the direction of travel, like the ball of an aircraft's turn-and-slip indicator. Centred, the turn is coordinated: the nose follows the track and the craft neither slips nor skids. The yaw is driven to keep it centred.
- **Thrust priority**: when Lift, Bank and Speed together need more thrust than the props can give, Lift is kept first, Bank second, and Speed gives way.

Mode 2 stick mapping:

| Stick | Channel | Function |
|---|---|---|
| Left U/D | throttle | **Speed**, not raw throttle. Lower half = a speed: bottom = zero speed (hard brake, and the pose for arming) up to `bank_mid_speed` (default 0.50 g of drag, about 20 m/s on the five inch) at the centre; only the bottom third brakes a craft that is faster than the stick's speed, above it the craft coasts down by its own drag. Upper half = a forward force: a straight line from the force the centre gives to all the (trimmed) thrust allows at the top, so full stick tilts as far as the thrust allows, as at full throttle in acro, and both modes reach the same top speed. The force over the stick is continuous at the centre. Forward tilt is limited only by the thrust; tilting back to brake by `bank_max_brake_angle` (30). No reverse. Speed is estimated from rotor drag (accelerometer) and attitude, in g of drag; control is feedforward + P (`bank_speed_kp`, `bank_speed_deadband`), so the bottom of the stick auto-brakes to a stop. Tilting forward also raises the thrust so Lift is unchanged |
| Left L/R | yaw | **Bank** left/right = turn in the horizontal plane. `bank_max_bank_angle` defaults to 90: full stick is as steep as the craft can turn, limited by the thrust reserve and by the coordinated turn the yaw can follow at the speed |
| Right U/D | pitch | **Lift**, the vertical force command. Bottom = no lift at all (free fall), centre = 1 g (thrust balances weight), top = maximum thrust entirely as lift, whatever multiple of the weight that is. Linear below the centre; above it `1 + x + (T/W - 2) x^3`, so both halves start at 1 g per half stick. The thrust-to-weight ratio is the fixed setting `bank_thrust_to_weight` (default 5.70, the simulator's five inch measured in a hover on a full pack), through a thrust curve `T = T/W * collective^1.5`; nothing is learnt in flight. The stick is a plain force with no vertical speed estimate: at the centre a climb or a sink carries on until the pilot stops it, and neither height nor vertical speed is held (the owner's decision, see "Lift stays a force") |
| Right L/R | roll | **head check**: yaw the nose away from the direction of travel *without* changing the direction of travel; springs back to neutral when the stick centres |

Design decisions so far:
- **Flight controls first, the look second** (owner, 2026-10-16): Speed, Lift and Bank always take priority over where the nose looks. The look into the turn and the head check may only rotate the view; they must never delay, weaken or fight what the flight controls ask for, and when the two compete (yaw authority, thrust, the attitude loop's attention) the flight controls win and the look waits. Check every change to the look against this.
- **Attitude only** (gyro/ACC); no GPS or baro required.
- Keep an internal virtual *travel heading* (a course reference, similar in spirit to headfree). Nose yaw = travel heading + head-check offset; the head-check offset must not affect the velocity direction.
- **Force-vector control (decoupled axes)**: the sticks command a net force vector, and the mixer of attitude + collective realises it. Required collective for tilt angle θ (pitch) and φ (bank) is T = (m·g + F_z) / (cos θ · cos φ), so with the right stick at the hover position, moving left U/D up from the bottom pitches the craft and increases throttle by 1/cos θ, giving forward force without changing vertical force. The right stick sets the vertical term F_z, with the bottom of the stick giving no lift. Arming is with the left stick at the bottom (zero speed, no tilt). Collective is therefore always computed from the tilt, never taken directly from the stick: from the measured tilt (since 2026-10-16), so Lift is right while the craft is still rotating to the commanded one.
- **Coordinated banked turn**: the yaw stick commands bank angle φ and the mode applies the same 1/cos tilt compensation so the vertical force stays balanced. The yaw is generated by a **"ball" loop** like the one in an aviation turn-and-slip indicator. tan φ = v·ω / g cannot be solved for ω without v, so instead of computing it, yaw is driven (P + I, with the sign flipped for rearward travel) to null the lateral specific force seen by the accelerometer, which on a multirotor is rotor drag and so a direct slip signal. The ball centred is the same condition as a perfect bank, with v eliminated and no velocity estimate. The settings are `bank_ball_kp`, `bank_ball_ki` and `bank_ball_deadband`.

## Implemented (2026-10-15)

Decisions made while flying it in the simulator, now in `flight/bank_mode.c`:

- **Thrust priority**: lift first, then bank, then forward speed. The tilt gets 75 % of the thrust above the vertical force; the rest is kept so the attitude loop still has motors to work with at the limit (without it a full bank at medium speed rolled over in the simulator).
- **Bank limit**: the bank is also capped at the steepest coordinated turn the yaw can follow, `tan(phi) <= 0.8 * bank_yaw_max_rate * v / turnGain`, never below 30 degrees.
- **Turn**: the yaw rate is fed forward from the bank, `omega = turnGain * tan(phi) / v` (`turnGain` learnt in steady turns from the ball integrator), and the ball loop (P + I) trims what is left. Bank is taken in the travel frame, before the head-check rotation.
- **Lift stays a force** (owner's decision, 2026-10-16): Lift is the vertical force and nothing else. Centre Lift is 1 g, zero vertical acceleration, so a climb or a sink carries on until the pilot stops it. No vertical speed estimate, no damping at the centre, no height hold. Considered and not taken: damping vertical speed at the centre (an accelerometer-integrated estimate, removed on 2026-10-15), Lift as a vertical speed command, and going back to the one-sided trim so the props' own damping returns. With the trim now matching the thrust to Lift, nothing else damps vertical speed either: the props make less thrust climbing and more sinking, and the trim takes that out too.
- **Speed: lower half a speed, upper half a forward force, brake in the bottom third** (2026-10-16): the owner found bank mode's top speed far below acro's (`webfpv-bank-20261016-165343.csv`: acro about 40 m/s at 50 to 65 degrees and full throttle; bank 19 to 22 m/s, the pitch target pinned at the 30 degree `bank_max_tilt_angle` with the motors near half). The forward tilt is now limited only by the thrust (with its 25 % reserve), and the old `bank_max_tilt_angle` became `bank_max_brake_angle`, which only limits tilting back to brake. The owner's design for the stick, from force over stick position:
  - lower half, a speed: 0 at the bottom to `bank_mid_speed` (new setting, default 0.50 g of drag) at the centre, feedforward + P. Only the bottom third (`BANK_BRAKE_STICK`) brakes: above it the P term only adds force, so a craft faster than the stick's speed gets the force that holds that speed and its own drag slows it, like coasting off the accelerator; a slower one is still driven up to it. Pulling the stick below a third when too fast is the brake.
  - upper half, a forward force: a straight line from the force the centre gives (the same P term, so the force over the stick is continuous at the centre whatever the speed) to the most forward force the trimmed thrust allows, lift * tan(tilt limit), at the top. No speed hold there.
  - How it got there: a first cut blended the top tenth of the stick to full force (the owner did not want the law to change near the top); then the speed was the stick times a `bank_max_speed` set above the top speed; then a mid speed with a computed top speed above it, where three quarters of the stick punched at near full power from the hover because the target sat above reach until the props lost thrust; the owner then chose a force for the upper half and braking only in the bottom third.
  - In the simulator, with Lift centred: full stick 40 m/s at 56 degrees, the same as a scripted acro pilot at full throttle (37 to 42 m/s); three quarters 35.5 m/s; half 19.6 m/s (0.43 g: the stick deadband and the P-only loop leave it short of 0.50); a quarter 6.8 m/s. From 40 m/s, the stick to 0.4 coasts to 20 m/s in 4 s and to its 14 m/s in about 10; to 0.3 brakes at 30 degrees to its 8.5 m/s in 2 s.
  - Still true at three quarters from the hover: the props have three times the forward force there that they have at top speed (4.4 g against 1.4 g), so a fraction of full force is a strong punch at first, as the same throttle is in acro. Raising the tilt cap alone flew the craft into the ground: at 40 m/s the props make a third of the modelled thrust, which needs the two-sided thrust trim below.
- **Collective from the measured tilt** (2026-10-16): Thrust = Lift / cos(measured tilt), never steeper than the thrust allows (so an attitude overshoot does not take the collective to full). It was set for the commanded tilt, and rolling into a bank at speed (the pitch target going from 50 to 0 degrees and the bank target from 0 to 80 at once) asked for the thrust of an 80 degree bank while the craft was still nearly level: 4 to 5 g up for a moment and 3 to 5 m/s more climb on every turn entry in the owner's flight `webfpv-bank-20261016-171738.csv`. Replayed through the new firmware, the turn entries add no climb. The thrust trim's model of the vertical force uses the measured tilt too.
- **Hard banks at speed** (2026-10-16, the owner's "janky between hard banks" flights `webfpv-bank-20261016-225549/230010/230106/230233.csv`): every flick of the Bank stick at speed nodded the pitch target 0 to 70 degrees and back, swung the nose 35 to 50 degrees off the track, kicked the climb up by 1.5 m/s and could drive the speed estimate negative (reversing). Fixed in this order, each measured with a scratch reversal sortie at 0.5 and 0.85 Speed:
  - the forward tilt is given what is left beside the steeper of the bank asked for and the bank the craft is still in (measured, travel frame), and it does not grow while the craft is rolling (the two banks more than 20 degrees apart), then at most 180 deg/s; it still falls at once (bank priority) and braking is not held. Pitch target spike through a reversal 70 degrees to 1.5, measured pitch excursion 38 to under 1.
  - the turn rate is fed forward from the bank the craft is in, not the one asked for: the track turns with the measured bank. The ball integrator and the turn-gain learner pause while rolling, as they do while the head check slews.
  - pid.c: under BANK the earth-referenced yaw is shared between body yaw and pitch by the measured attitude, not the angle targets (the target is already on the other side while the craft rolls through, and pitched the nose up 15 to 25 degrees).
  - the drag is levelled with each sample's attitude before the 2 Hz filter, not after (rolling from 50 degrees of forward tilt into a hard bank read as a 0.4 g slip for a quarter of a second).
  - the thrust the trim adds is taken as proportional to the inflow through the props (speed times the sine of the forward tilt) and shrinks with it at once: rolling from a fast forward run into a bank gave the props their thrust back faster than the 3 Hz trim saw it. Climb per hard turn entered at speed 1.5 m/s to about 0.2.
  - tilted forward more than 15 degrees the craft is not reversing whatever the drag says (a fast climb levels as a tailwind): the speed reads no less than 0 and the ball keeps its forward sense.
  - `bank_ball_kp` and `bank_ball_ki` 300 to 600: steady slip in a held full bank 12 to 9 degrees, no oscillation seen.
  - Owner's four logs replayed: half seconds with pitch swings 16/27/35/34 in the flights to 6/16/20/15; what is left is mostly Lift flicks (full Lift takes all the thrust vertical, so the forward tilt drops to 0 at once: Lift first) and braking.
  - Still true: in a held full bank the speed bleeds off (about 20 m/s in 2.5 s from 37 m/s), because the bank takes the whole tilt budget and Speed gives way (thrust priority as decided); the pitch spikes had been topping the speed up. Above about 70 degrees of bank the ball cannot see a slip: body Y is nearly vertical, so the slip is air through the disc, and a climb of 1 m/s reads as much as a 10 degree slip. Pitched and banked together at speed (44 and 60 degrees) the quadratic body drag makes the levelling over-subtract, and the ball centres 15 degrees off the track.
- **Looking into the turn** (2026-10-16, reworked the same day): the owner turns in acro with the nose into the turn, not along the track. In `webfpv-bank-20261016-234544.csv` (track rebuilt from the world-frame accelerometer, size pinned to the logged speed), turns at 14 to 35 m/s had the camera (30 degree uptilt) a median 76 degrees into the turn from the track (middle half 60 to 106) and the thrust 67 degrees in; straight runs looked 10 degrees off the track. The turn is flown as a car on a banked track (nose along the track, the bank as roll), and the mode adds a head check of its own on top, set by the sticks alone: `offset = -sign(bank) * atan2(|bank|, speed) * min(|bank| / 0.2, 1)`, from the shaped Bank and Speed sticks, slewed at the head check rate, with the head check stick on top. Full Speed with a little Bank looks about 11 degrees in, full Speed with full Bank 45, half Speed with full Bank 63, no Speed with a big Bank 90; the fade over the first 20 % of the Bank stick keeps a nudge at low Speed from swinging the nose round. The thrust in the world frame, and so the track, is unchanged. The head check counts as slewing only while its rate limit holds it back, so the ball integrator and the turn gain learner still run while the look moves. The owner found it "feeling ok" (2026-10-17); see "Refine the look into the turn" below.
  - Sortie (0.5 Speed, full Bank): nose 31 degrees into the turn, body roll 12 and pitch 54, the roll target steady, coordination 0.05 deg/s. `turn_ball_g` fails at -0.86 (it averages the raw body Y accelerometer, which lies partly along the track with the nose off it; the firmware's own ball, `DEBUG_BANK` 6, is in the travel frame); `turn_slip_deg` reads the look, -31.
  - Before it, two looks from the thrust direction, both replaced: (1) from the commanded travel-frame tilt, `-atan2(cos(theta) sin(phi), max(sin(theta), 0)) * min(|phi| / 20 deg, 1)` (commit 57913f4): it rocked on entry and swung back on exit (see "The look rocks, snaps and jitters" below); (2) from the measured tilt seen from the track, so the nose would follow the real thrust: the tilt is read through the offset the nose is meant to be at, so a nose lagging the offset reads as thrust further into the turn and the look ran ahead of it. Unfiltered (and at 2 Hz) the sortie's turn rocked the roll target +-40 degrees at 1.6 Hz; at 0.5 Hz it settled, but in the owner's `webfpv-bank-20261017-014805.csv` the yaw sat at its 360 deg/s limit for 22 % of the hard-bank frames while the craft yawed 120 to 230 deg/s, and the roll target was more than 20 degrees on the wrong side of the bank for 20 % of them.
- **Speed estimate**: in g of drag, from the accelerometer drag levelled with the measured attitude, normalised by sqrt(thrust) (rotor drag grows with the induced velocity), the thrust being the trimmed one (the untrimmed model said three times the real thrust at 40 m/s, so the speed read 0.6 g there instead of 1.0). A climb or a sink with the craft tilted also puts air across the rotors, which reads as speed and slip; with no vertical speed estimate it is not taken out.
- **Hooks**: BANK turns ANGLE_MODE on under it (like ALT_HOLD), so the PID treats it as a levelled mode; it overrides the angle targets, the yaw setpoint and the collective. Under BANK the roll and pitch sticks give no rate setpoint (they are lift and head check).
- **Accelerometer units**: the mode takes 1 g as the accelerometer's reading at rest; there is no calibration. The simulator flies the five inch at 1.62 g and reports its accelerometer in that local g.
- **Diagnostics**: `debug_mode = BANK` logs speed and target speed, thrust trim, thrust to weight, turn gain, ball and vertical force command (`DEBUG_SET` calls in `bank_mode.c`).

Known open points: the scripted sortie flies 0.5 Speed, which is 19.5 m/s since `bank_mid_speed` (it was about 10 m/s), and one row fails there: `turn_ball_g` -0.211 (it averages the raw body Y accelerometer, and at 26 degrees of pitch in the 52 degree turn the forward drag reads about -0.27 on that axis in a coordinated turn; the firmware's own ball levels it out and reads about 0). `headreturn_nose_offset_deg` passes since the hard-bank fixes (2.9, was 6.6, band 5). Flown at 0.27 Speed (9 m/s, the old cruise), every row passes but `headcheck_track_change_deg` -11.1 (band 10, -10.1 before). The owner decides whether the sortie's Speed or the rows change. The head check rotates the tilt by the commanded offset while the nose lags it at 360 deg/s, so the track moves about 10 degrees on a full head check; `core.c` resets I-term when the throttle stick is low and airmode has not latched, and under BANK the throttle stick is the speed command (needs a BANK exception before flying hardware); the speed has no authority during a full bank (lowest priority), so the turn speed depends on the entry.

## To look at

Noted by the owner on 2026-10-15 for a later pass. Each works in the simulator today, but is a first cut.

- **`bank_thrust_to_weight` is hard-set, the thrust-to-weight learner is removed: come back to this** (2026-10-15). The default is 5.70, measured from the owner's flight `webfpv-bank-20261016-020102.csv`: a 12 s hover at the centre of the lift stick held 1.000 g at collective 0.3135 on a full pack (25.06 V under load), and 1 / 0.3135^1.5 = 5.70. The learner (the earth-vertical accelerometer over collective^1.5, 0.2 Hz, while the stick asked for 1 to 3 g, within a factor 2.5 of the setting, reset on disarm) is gone for now, so the centre of the lift stick is 1 g only in a hover on a full pack. Known and accepted for now:
  - **Battery sag**: thrust goes roughly with the pack voltage squared, and the owner's packs sag from 25.6 V to 21.8 V in a flight, about 25 to 30 % less thrust at the end. The scripted sortie flew at 4.0 V a cell and the centre stick gave only 0.9 g (the sortie now flies a full pack, 4.2 V, as the shell starts). Betaflight's `vbat_sag_compensation` would take most of this out.
  - **Airflow**: the props make less thrust per collective climbing and in fast forward flight. In the owner's logs the learnt value was 5.5 to 5.9 hovering, 4.1 to 4.8 climbing and 4.5 to 5.0 level at 20 to 30 m/s. Before 2026-10-16, at full speed stick with the lift stick centred the craft sank at 0.4 m/s at 10 m/s and 1.8 m/s at 19 m/s. The thrust trim now adds the lost thrust back (see "Thrust trim adds thrust too").
  - Ideas when we come back: turn on `vbat_sag_compensation`; measure the hover value at the start of a flight (or save it on disarm) instead of hard-setting it; a short punch to 2 g for a second point on the thrust curve, to check `collective^1.5` (and relate it to Betaflight's `thrust_linear`); a learner held within about 30 % of the measured value for the airflow effects.
- **Lift stick as a plain force** (changed 2026-10-15): the vertical speed estimate (earth-vertical accelerometer integrated with a 60 s leak) and the damping it drove at the centre of the lift stick (0.1 g per m/s, at most 0.5 g) were removed, so the stick is bottom 0 g, centre 1 g (from `bank_thrust_to_weight`), top full lift. In the owner's flights the damping only slowed a centre-stick climb at about 2 m/s² instead of 1, and the estimate read about 0.6 of the true speed in the simulator (it integrates in Earth g, the sim's accelerometer reads its local 1.62 g). Cost: the estimate also took the climb's share out of the rotor drag; without it a 3.5 m/s climb through a 52 degree turn in the scripted sortie slips 22 degrees instead of 13 and the speed runs up from 10 to 16 m/s. Open: climbs and sinks at centre stick after braking and in fast forward flight (seen in the owner's logs), and whether a baro-backed vertical speed should come back for the drag correction only.
- **Thrust trim adds thrust too** (2026-10-16): while the craft reads below 1 g (it cannot be resting on the ground then), less vertical force than modelled is taken as thrust lost and the trim adds it back, down to a factor of 0.2; at or above 1 g it only goes back up towards 1, and a trim below 1 leaks back over 5 s instead of 1 s. At 40 m/s the five inch's props make about a third of the modelled thrust (trim 0.35), and without this full Speed flew into the ground in a few seconds. The bank and forward tilt limits use the trimmed thrust, and so does the drag normalisation of the speed estimate (the untrimmed model said three times the real thrust at 40 m/s, and the speed read 0.6 g there instead of 1.0). Costs: a centre-Lift climb or sink carries on instead of dying out by itself (accepted, see "Lift stays a force"), and the climb reads as slip (`turn_slip_deg` 19 degrees at 0.27 Speed with the sortie's take-off climb carried through the turn, 6.7 before; info row, the ball still centres). Open: the leak leaves a slow sink at full Speed (about -0.01 g, -2.6 m/s after 10 s at 40 m/s); a vertical speed for the drag correction only would take the climb's share out of the slip.
- **Fast thrust trim** (added 2026-10-15): with the lift stick a plain force, quick balloons at centre stick stayed as steady climbs. Braking from speed the rotors tilt back into the air and make up to 1.6 times the modelled thrust, and rolling into a bank the collective rises by 1/cos at once while the craft is still level. The trim compares the measured vertical force with what the collective was set for at the measured tilt (the commanded one until 2026-10-16) (3 Hz, motor lag modelled at 10 Hz) and divides the thrust by it. It only takes thrust off (on the ground with the stick up it would otherwise launch the craft on lift-off) and leaks back to 1 over 1 s (on the ground any trim measures as right, and one held off 1 walked the thrust-to-weight learner, since removed, to its limit). In the simulator, braking from 23 m/s at centre stick climbed +4.2 m/s without it and peaks at +0.75 m/s with it; a full bank entered at 20 m/s climbed +8.5 m/s and now +3.9 m/s. These numbers were measured with the thrust-to-weight learner still in. Since 2026-10-16 it also adds thrust while the craft is sinking (see above).
- **Power in reserve**: bank and forward tilt get 75 % of the thrust above the vertical force (`BANK_TILT_THRUST_SHARE`). A/B in the simulator showed it is needed (without it a full bank at medium speed rolled over), but the value is a fixed guess. Look at making it a setting, or deriving it from the motor headroom the mixer reports (`getMotorMixRange`), and at its interaction with airmode and throttle boost.
- **Refine the look into the turn** (owner, 2026-10-17: the stick look feels ok, look at it later): the offset is a first cut from the stick ratio alone (above). To look at:
  - The shape: how far it looks for each Bank and Speed (scale, a maximum below 90 degrees, a curve rather than atan2), maybe as settings; whether it should follow what the craft is doing (speed, bank reached) rather than the sticks, without the feedback that ran away in the measured-tilt look.
  - Timing: the look jumps with the stick at the head check rate, ahead of the roll-in, so on a hard entry the bank is rotated before the nose has turned (the entry rock above); on a release or a reversal it swings straight to the new side.
  - Low speed: where the turn needs more yaw than `bank_yaw_max_rate` (8 to 14 m/s in hard banks), the nose falls behind the offset while the tilt is still rotated by it, so part of the bank shows as body roll the wrong way. Cut the look's slew while the yaw is at its limit, so the turn gets the yaw first ("flight controls first").
  - The sortie's `turn_ball_g` row measures the body Y accelerometer, which no longer means the ball with the nose off the track; measure it in the travel frame. A hard bank at low Speed is 60 to 85 degrees of pitch, close to the angle loop's 85 degree Euler limit in pid.c.
- **The look rocks, snaps and jitters in hard banks** (owner's flights of 2026-10-16, on the committed look): (1) on a hard entry the tilt is rotated by the look as it slews (360 deg/s) while the nose lags it 30 to 40 degrees, so the bank is flown as body roll first and turns into pitch as the nose comes round: the roll target rose to 69 degrees and fell to 0 within 0.15 s, the craft rolled in 33 and rocked back (`webfpv-bank-20261017-002232.csv`, 8.3 s); (2) letting go of a hard bank, the bank comes off at once and the look swings 90 degrees back to the track, against the turn just flown, with a pitch-up of about 70 degrees at the same moment (167 to 325 deg/s of yaw the wrong way on every exit of `webfpv-bank-20261017-004932.csv`); (3) at 70 to 80 degrees of tilt the body roll is very sensitive to the nose (10 degrees of nose is 35 of roll), so any unevenness in the look shows as large roll swings in the view.
  - Tried on 2026-10-16 and reverted (owner: worse to fly; the diff is not kept in the repo): (a) holding the tilt back until the nose had turned, scaled by the cosine of the look still to go: no roll-in, but the bank waited for the nose (reversals level for 0.2 s), against "flight controls first"; (b) bank first, the look slower (180 deg/s), the tilt rotated by where the nose really is (a nose lag from the gyro heading rate `(q sin(roll) + r cos(roll)) / cos(pitch)`, which matched the plant's yaw, caught up at 5/s): faster turns, but the full bank comes as roll and unrolls as the nose arrives, a bigger swing than the rock; (c) on top of (b), the look held while rolling out and returned gently, eased towards its target as a first order lag: exits and jitter measured better on replays, but the owner found the flying bad. The common problem is the look itself at steep tilt, not its timing.
- **Nose dive with Lift at the bottom** (owner's `webfpv-bank-20261017-004932.csv`, 2026-10-16): with Lift near the bottom and Speed at the centre, the pitch target goes to 80 to 85 degrees nose down with no bank asked (6.8 to 7.1 s and 7.9 to 8.4 s), and the craft follows it to 81. `bankForceToTilt()` divides the Speed stick's forward force by the lift, which is next to nothing there, so the forward tilt asked for goes towards 90 degrees, though with the collective at 0 it makes no force at all. Look at what the tilt should be when there is no lift to tilt (hold the attitude, or limit the forward tilt by the lift).
- **Banking at low speed**: what Bank should do slow or from the hover. In a replay at 8 to 11 m/s sinking at 12 m/s (with change (b) above in), the turn's own yaw (feedforward and ball) saturated at 360 deg/s and flipped between +360 and -360 from one update to the next. Today the bank is limited to the coordinated turn the yaw can follow (`bankCoordinatedBankLimit()`, never below 30 degrees), the turn feedforward fades out below 0.1 g of drag, and the look turns the nose up to 90 degrees into the turn at low Speed, so a bank from the hover is mostly a sideways push with the nose swung round.
- **Vertical velocity based controls, again**: re-explore Lift (or its centre) as a vertical speed rather than a plain force, or a vertical speed for the drag correction only. This reopens "Lift stays a force" and the removed accelerometer-integrated estimate ("Lift stick as a plain force" above), so start from why those were dropped: the estimate read about 0.6 of the true speed in the simulator, and the damping did little. Climbs and sinks carried on at centre stick are still in the owner's logs.
- **Thrust angle limit**: there is no single cap on the total tilt. The bank is limited by the thrust reserve and the coordinated-turn limit, forward tilt by the thrust reserve only (braking by `bank_max_brake_angle`), and only the pid.c hook's 85 degree angle limit bounds them together; with the lift stick below the centre the reserve allows steeper. Look at a total tilt limit (bank and pitch together), as a setting like `angle_limit`.
